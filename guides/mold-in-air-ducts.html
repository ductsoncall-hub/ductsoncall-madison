Mold in air ducts: what it means, what testing shows, and what actually works

By Nick Bellino, NADCA ASCS · Ducts On Call, Madison WI · Updated 2026-10-23

Mold in a duct system is a moisture story first. Finding it is a reason to look for water, not just a reason to clean.

Where it grows

Sheet metal ducts are a poor surface for mold unless they are dirty and damp. The usual locations are the air conditioner's evaporator coil and drain pan, the plenum directly after it, insulated or lined ducts that have gotten wet, and returns in damp basements. Dust is the food; condensation or a leak is the water.

How to tell

Discoloration alone proves little; many deposits look like mold. The EPA suggests having a sample analyzed by a laboratory if you are unsure. Indoor air and surface sampling can also tell you whether spore levels indoors differ from outdoors, and mycotoxin testing adds another layer where sensitive occupants are involved. Nick tests and reports what the results show; he does not diagnose health conditions.

What does not work

Spraying a biocide or sealant over dirty ductwork. The EPA notes that no chemical biocides are registered for use in bare sheet metal ducts, and sealants applied over debris trap it rather than remove it. If the moisture source is not fixed, growth comes back.

What does

  1. Fix the water: coil drainage, insulation, humidity, leaks.
  2. Mechanically remove the debris under negative pressure, so the food source is gone.
  3. Clean the coil, pan and blower.
  4. Where growth was confirmed, a bio-growth treatment appropriate for the surface.
  5. Re-test if the stakes warrant it.
